# The Anishinaabemowin partnership — round three

## The model

The standing partnership model
(`docs/i18n-partnership-cherokee.md`, argued in full there,
including the track-wide never-scaffold-unbidden policy):
**Anishinaabe speakers translate, we supply all tooling, and
nothing ships before their review** — the whole technical burden
on us; no deadline, any pace; credit, review authority, and
unshipping on request theirs; compensation discussed honestly in
the first conversation; licensing and materials ownership
settled in writing first. The scaffold is
`docs/i18n-glossary/oj.md`, which inherits the Cree track's
continuum posture: "would YOUR Anishinaabemowin belong here?" —
with room for more than one answer.

## Why Anishinaabemowin

- A revitalization movement of unusual energy and infrastructure:
  immersion schools (Waadookodaading in Wisconsin, Niigaane at
  Leech Lake), the elder-recorded **Ojibwe People's Dictionary**,
  university programs on both sides of the border, and Canadian
  Anishinabek education institutions.
- The concept fit is the plainest on the entire track: an
  immersion school literally named "**the place where people
  help each other**" — the app's function is already a named
  Anishinaabe practice (wiidookodaadiwin), and the scaffold
  flags it as the anchor to confirm.
- As with Cree, the continuum means the first engaging community
  sets the first path, and others can follow as separate
  registry entries.

## Who to contact

- **Waadookodaading Ojibwe Language Institute** (Hayward, WI) —
  the immersion school whose name says it all.
- **Niigaane Ojibwemowin Immersion School** (Leech Lake, MN).
- **The Ojibwe People's Dictionary team** (University of
  Minnesota, with its elder speakers) — conventions and
  terminology.
- **Seven Generations Education Institute** (Treaty #3
  territory, ON) and **Anishinabek Nation** education programs.
- Tribal colleges of the region (Lac Courte Oreilles, Fond du
  Lac, Red Lake Nation College).

## Draft outreach note (personalize before sending)

> Subject: An invitation — a mutual-aid app's interface in
> Anishinaabemowin, on your terms
>
> Boozhoo — my name is [name]. I help maintain Understoria, a
> free, open-source mutual-aid platform: neighbors exchange help
> hour-for-hour, communities run their own servers, there are no
> ads, no tracking, and no company behind it. It speaks
> seventeen languages today, and we run a partnership-first
> translation track for Indigenous languages — the same
> invitation has gone to Cherokee, Navajo, Lakota, Cree,
> Inuktitut, Choctaw, Quechua, Māori, and Mohawk programs.
>
> I'm writing to ask whether an interface in Anishinaabemowin —
> in your dialect and writing system — is something your program
> would find worthwhile. We learned while preparing this that
> one of your immersion schools is named "the place where people
> help each other" — which is, word for word, what this app is
> for; it seemed like an invitation to ask. The offer is the
> opposite of the usual one: we are not asking permission to
> machine-translate the language, and we won't do that. The
> technical groundwork is done for both the double-vowel
> orthography and syllabics, we've documented the decisions that
> proved hard in seventeen other languages, and the ask is
> whether Anishinaabe speakers — your teachers, your students,
> whoever you'd choose — would want to do the actual
> translation, at whatever pace works, with review authority
> over every word, ownership settled in writing first, credit
> however you want it, and compensation discussed honestly up
> front. Nothing ships until you say it's right; there is no
> deadline; and if a different community's variety is the better
> fit, we'd welcome the redirection.
>
> If this has any place in your language work — or if it
> doesn't, but you'd point me somewhere better — I'd be glad for
> a short conversation. Miigwech for reading this, and for the
> work you do.
>
> [name, contact, link to the project]
