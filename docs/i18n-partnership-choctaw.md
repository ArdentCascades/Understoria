# The Choctaw partnership — round two begins

## The model

The standing partnership model, argued in full in
`docs/i18n-partnership-cherokee.md` and applied to every track
since: **Choctaw speakers translate, we supply all tooling, and
nothing ships before their review.** The offer is unchanged and
stated plainly: the whole technical burden on us; no deadline,
any pace; credit, review authority, and unshipping on request
theirs; compensation discussed honestly in the first
conversation; licensing and materials ownership settled in
writing before work starts. The scaffold is
`docs/i18n-glossary/cho.md`.

## Why Choctaw opens round two

- **Institutional readiness**: the Choctaw Nation's School of
  Choctaw Language runs standing online and community classes —
  an organized body of teachers and curriculum writers who are
  exactly the "whoever you'd choose" this model needs — and the
  Mississippi Band's communities carry some of the strongest
  first-language transmission in the United States.
- **A literacy tradition two centuries deep**: Choctaw was among
  the earliest Native languages written and printed in the U.S.,
  with its own newspapers and a hymnody in continuous use — the
  question here is invitation, not possibility.
- **The register ground writes itself**: the 1847 Choctaw gift to
  famine Ireland — help sent level, across an ocean, by people
  sixteen years past their own removal — is the plainest
  statement of this app's whole premise anywhere in history, and
  it is already Choctaw.

## Who to contact

- **Choctaw Nation of Oklahoma — School of Choctaw Language**
  (Durant, OK) — the largest program and the natural first door.
- **Mississippi Band of Choctaw Indians — language and culture
  programs** (Choctaw, MS) — the homeland communities with the
  strongest daily use of the language.
- **Jena Band of Choctaw Indians** (Louisiana).

As with every track: if more than one program engages, each
should know of the others, and the Cherokee/Navajo/Lakota/Cree/
Inuktitut tracks' lessons flow here too.

>

> to Cherokee, Navajo, Lakota, Cree, and Inuktitut programs.
>
> I'm writing to ask whether an interface in Choctaw — in the
> spelling conventions your program uses — is something you would
> find worthwhile. The offer is the opposite of the usual one: we
> are not asking permission to machine-translate the language,
> and we won't do that. The technical groundwork is done, we've
> documented the decisions that proved hard in seventeen other
> languages, and the ask is whether Choctaw speakers — your
> teachers, your students, whoever you'd choose — would want to
> do the actual translation, at whatever pace works, with review
> authority over every word, ownership settled in writing first,
> credit however you want it, and compensation discussed honestly
> up front. Nothing ships until you say it's right; there is no
> deadline on our side.
>

> premise is neighbors helping level, with nothing owed, knows
> exactly one perfect historical statement of itself — the gift

> have the app speak the language that did that.
>
> If this has any place in your language work — or if it doesn't,
> but you'd point me somewhere better — I'd be glad for a short
> conversation. Yakoke for reading this, and for the work you do.
