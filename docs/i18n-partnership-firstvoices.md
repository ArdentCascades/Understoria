# The FirstVoices / FPCC umbrella — one conversation, many nations

## What this document is, and deliberately is not

This is a partnership document **with no language scaffold
attached, by design.** British Columbia holds more Indigenous
language diversity than the rest of Canada combined — on the
order of thirty-four languages across seven families (Salishan,
Wakashan, Tsimshianic, Dene, Algonquian, and the Haida and
Ktunaxa isolates) — and the First Peoples' Cultural Council
(FPCC), a First Nations-governed Crown corporation, stewards
revitalization across all of them, including the
community-controlled **FirstVoices** platform (language archives,
keyboards, and apps built with and owned by the communities
themselves).

The right form of outreach here is therefore **one conversation
at the umbrella, not thirty-four scaffolds**: FPCC and the
FirstVoices teams know which communities might want an interface,
which are focused elsewhere, and which hold their languages in
ways that make the question itself inappropriate. Per the
track-wide policy recorded in
`docs/i18n-partnership-cherokee.md`, **no BC language gets a
scaffold, a published probe, or a candidate-list entry unless a
community chooses it through channels like these.** This
document exists so the door is visibly open and the model is
legible — nothing more.

## What we would offer any community FPCC connects us with

The standing model, unchanged: speakers translate, we supply all
tooling, nothing ships before their review; no deadline, any
pace; credit, review authority, and unshipping on request theirs;
compensation discussed honestly; licensing and materials
ownership settled in writing first — a posture that should sound
familiar, because community ownership of language materials is
FirstVoices' own founding principle. Our tooling would
interoperate with, never duplicate or ingest, FirstVoices
archives: their data stays theirs, under their control, full
stop.

Two technical notes we can offer up front, from the sibling
tracks' verified groundwork:

- BC orthographies' machine-hostile characters are a solved
  problem for us in principle: the sibling scaffolds already
  handle syllabics, combining marks, modifier letters, and even
  punctuation-as-letters (Kanien'kéha's length colon) — and BC
  conventions like the digit 7 as a glottal stop (Sḵwx̱wú7mesh)
  or underline and caron diacritics are the same *kind* of
  problem: one orthography check and a gate that never "fixes"
  a letter as a typo. FirstVoices' own keyboards are the
  authority we would follow.
- Most BC languages will be absent from CLDR/ICU; our no-ICU
  pattern (fallback-safe plurals, honest date disclosure) has
  shipped and is documented in the sibling scaffolds.

## Who to contact

- **First Peoples' Cultural Council** (Brentwood Bay, BC) — the
  umbrella; its language department and the FirstVoices team.
- Through FPCC, as *they* judge appropriate — never directly and
  cold from us — individual nations' language authorities and
  programs.
