# The Inuktitut partnership — the track's fifth language

## The model (shared with Cherokee, Navajo, Lakota, and Cree)

This document extends the partnership track
(`docs/i18n-partnership-cherokee.md`, argued in full there) to
Inuktitut — the Inuit-Yupik-Unangan family's entry, closing the
first round of the one-language-per-family plan. In brief:

**Inuit speakers translate, we supply all tooling, and nothing
ships before their review.** The offer is the standing one,
stated plainly rather than assumed: the whole technical burden on
us; no deadline, any pace; credit and review authority theirs,
including the words the Settings language card uses and
unshipping on request; compensation discussed honestly in the
first conversation; licensing and materials ownership discussed
before work starts, in writing.

## What is different about Inuktitut

- **It is an official language with a statutory language
  authority.** Nunavut's laws require public services in Inuktut,
  and Inuit Uqausinginnik Taiguusiliuqtiit exists precisely to
  decide terminology questions. On every other track we ask who
  could decide; here an institution's mandate already covers it.
  Our posture adapts accordingly: the partnership can run through
  official channels from the start if that is the right path.
- **Localization precedent is the deepest on the track**: Windows
  and Office shipped in Inuktitut through Government of Nunavut
  collaboration; Microsoft Translator supports Inuktitut in both
  scripts. We say the second part honestly: machine translation
  exists here, unusually — and it changes nothing about this
  track's model, because the register decisions this app lives on
  are craft, not coverage, and authority rests with speakers
  either way.
- **The script decision is national and live**: syllabics
  (predominant in Nunavut and Nunavik) versus Roman orthographies
  (western dialects, Nunatsiavut) versus the unified **Inuktut
  Qaliujaaqpait** Roman orthography that Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami
  adopted in 2019 for all dialects — an Inuit-led transition in
  progress. The interface follows the partners' choice, and the
  registry can carry a second script entry later if wanted.
- **The technical ground (verified, `docs/i18n-glossary/iu.md`)**:
  native CLDR plural rules **with a grammatical dual** — the
  track's first, and our gates already sanction natural dual
  forms via the Arabic precedent — while dates and numbers fall
  back harmlessly; both scripts verified rendering-safe with the
  font plan recorded; Inuinnaqtun can join later as its own entry
  on the proven no-ICU pattern.
- **The register ground includes a living analog for the app's
  oldest template**: the community freezer — organized
  country-food sharing — is exactly the community-fridge pattern,
  already named in every community. And the counting register
  carries the E-number discs within living memory, which makes
  this app's identity model (a name you choose, keys you hold, no
  rosters) land with particular force — a resonance the scaffold
  holds through every enrollment and identity string.

## Who to contact

The person sending this does not live on Inuit Nunangat, and the
note below is written accordingly.

- **Pirurvik Centre** (Iqaluit) — the Inuktitut language,
  culture, and wellbeing centre that carried the Microsoft-era
  localization work; the most direct experience on earth with
  exactly this task.
- **Inuit Uqausinginnik Taiguusiliuqtiit** — Nunavut's Inuit
  Language Authority; the statutory terminology body.
- **Inuit Tapiriit Kanatami** — the national organization, and
  steward of the unified orthography; the right door for the
  script question and for pan-Inuit framing.
- **Nunavut Arctic College** — Inuktitut programs and teacher
  education.
- **Avataq Cultural Institute** (Nunavik) — for the
  Quebec-region dialect and a possible fr-first fallback
  decision.

If several engage — or the four sibling tracks have by then —
each should know of the others; the scaffolds cross-reference and
lessons flow both ways.
