# The Māori partnership — and what this track can learn from it

## The model

The standing partnership model
(`docs/i18n-partnership-cherokee.md`, argued in full there):
**Māori speakers translate, we supply all tooling, and nothing
ships before their review** — the whole technical burden on us;
no deadline, any pace; credit, review authority, and unshipping
on request theirs; compensation discussed honestly in the first
conversation; licensing and materials ownership settled in
writing first. The scaffold is `docs/i18n-glossary/mi.md`.

## Why Māori — and why this track owes this community already

Te reo Māori is on this list for a reason beyond the
translation: **the principles this entire track runs on were
articulated most clearly by Māori organizations.** Kōhanga reo
invented the language-nest model that revitalization movements
worldwide adopted — including programs our other tracks contact.
And Te Hiku Media built speech technology for te reo on
community-controlled data under a guardianship license, declining
extractive partnerships and stating plainly that language data is
a taonga under kaitiakitanga. Our review-before-ship posture is,
in effect, that principle applied to a translation pipeline. So
this outreach carries an extra, honest line: alongside the
translation invitation, we would welcome their critique of the
track itself — five North American packages and two hemispheric
siblings would be better for ten minutes of their skepticism.

The practical ground is also the strongest anywhere: official-
language status with a statutory commission (Te Taura Whiri i te
Reo Māori), a full immersion-education pipeline, settled
orthography, real localization precedent — and (verified) a
formats-strong CLDR profile that renders te reo dates for free.
Machine translation exists in the major systems; the standing
note applies — it changes nothing, register is craft, authority
rests with speakers.

## Who to contact

- **Te Taura Whiri i te Reo Māori** (the Māori Language
  Commission) — the statutory authority; terminology conventions
  live here.
- **Te Hiku Media** (Kaitaia) — for the data-sovereignty
  conversation as much as the translation one; if they only ever
  reviewed our model, the contact would be worth it.
- **Te Mātāwai** — the revitalization strategy entity.
- **Te Ipukarea** (the National Māori Language Institute, AUT)
  and the wānanga (Te Wānanga o Raukawa, Te Wānanga o Aotearoa,
  Awanuiārangi) — teaching institutions with translation
  capacity.

> Subject: An invitation — a mutual-aid app's interface in te
> reo, on your terms — and your critique of how we're doing this
>
> Tēnā koutou — my name is [name]. I help maintain Understoria,
> a free, open-source mutual-aid platform: neighbors exchange
> help hour-for-hour, communities run their own servers, there
> are no ads, no tracking, and no company behind it. It speaks
> seventeen languages today, and we run a partnership-first
> translation track for Indigenous and marginalized languages —
> the same invitation has gone to Cherokee, Navajo, Lakota,
> Cree, Inuktitut, Choctaw, and Quechua programs.
>
> Two asks, honestly separated. The first is the invitation:
> whether an interface in te reo Māori is something your
> organization would find worthwhile — speakers you choose doing
> the actual translation, at whatever pace works, with review
> authority over every word, ownership settled in writing first,
> credit however you want it, and compensation discussed
> honestly up front; we will not machine-translate the language,
> whatever tools exist, and nothing ships until you say it's
> right.
>
> The second ask is smaller and maybe more valuable to us: the
> principles this track runs on — community authority over
> language, nothing shipped without speaker review, data as
> taonga — we learned in no small part from Māori organizations'
> public example. If you ever had ten minutes to tell us where
> our model still falls short, we would take that critique as
> seriously as any translation.
>
> If either has a place in your work — or neither does, but
> you'd point us somewhere better — I'd be glad for a short
> conversation. Ngā mihi nui, and thank you for the work you do.
>
> [name, contact, link to the project]
