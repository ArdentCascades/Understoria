# The Mohawk partnership — round three

## The model

The standing partnership model
(`docs/i18n-partnership-cherokee.md`, argued in full there,
including the track-wide never-scaffold-unbidden policy):
**Kanien'kéha speakers translate, we supply all tooling, and
nothing ships before their review** — the whole technical burden
on us; no deadline, any pace; credit, review authority, and
unshipping on request theirs; compensation discussed honestly in
the first conversation; licensing and materials ownership settled
in writing first. The scaffold is `docs/i18n-glossary/moh.md`.

## Why Kanien'kéha

A second Iroquoian language, earned on its own merits: the
revitalization movement is among the most sustained anywhere —
the Akwesasne Freedom School has run immersion since 1979,
Onkwawenna Kentyohkwa's adult program at Six Nations is a model
studied worldwide, and Kahnawà:ke's language center anchors a
community that legislated for its language early. The people
this model needs — speakers who teach, and institutions that
back them — already exist in numbers. And the app's architecture
has a resonance here it has nowhere else: a federation of
communities on parallel courses, none steering another, serving
one nation split by an international border.

>
> Shé:kon — my name is [name]. I help maintain Understoria, a
> free, open-source mutual-aid platform: neighbors exchange help

> ads, no tracking, and no company behind it. It speaks
> seventeen languages today, and we run a partnership-first
> translation track for Indigenous languages — the same
> invitation has gone to Cherokee, Navajo, Lakota, Cree,
> Inuktitut, Choctaw, Quechua, and Māori programs.
>
> I'm writing to ask whether an interface in Kanien'kéha — in
> your community's conventions — is something your program would
> find worthwhile. The offer is the opposite of the usual one:
> we are not asking permission to machine-translate the
> language, and we won't do that. The technical groundwork is
> done (including the small things machines get wrong, like
> treating the length colon as a letter), we've documented the
> decisions that proved hard in seventeen other languages, and
> the ask is whether Kanien'kéha speakers — your teachers, your
> students, whoever you'd choose — would want to do the actual
> translation, at whatever pace works, with review authority
> over every word, ownership settled in writing first, credit
> however you want it, and compensation discussed honestly up
> front. Nothing ships until you say it's right; there is no
> deadline on our side.
>
> If this has any place in your language work — or if it
> doesn't, but you'd point me somewhere better — I'd be glad for
> a short conversation. Niá:wen for reading this, and for the
> work you do.
