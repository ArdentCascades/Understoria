# The Quechua partnership — the track goes hemispheric

## The model

The standing partnership model
(`docs/i18n-partnership-cherokee.md`, argued in full there):
**Quechua speakers translate, we supply all tooling, and nothing
ships before their review** — with the standing offer stated
plainly: the whole technical burden on us; no deadline, any
pace; credit, review authority, and unshipping on request
theirs; compensation discussed honestly in the first
conversation; licensing and materials ownership settled in
writing first. The scaffold is `docs/i18n-glossary/qu.md`.

## Why Quechua, and why now

- **The app already half-speaks the region**: Understoria ships
  complete Spanish — every string, playbook, and guide — which
  becomes both the members' fallback (`qu → es → en`, the
  strongest case yet for a non-uniform chain) and a working
  bridge for translators, who can consult polished Spanish
  alongside English for every line. No other track has that.
- **The culture names the app's concepts**: *ayni*, *mink'a*,
  *ayllu* — reciprocity, the communal work day, the community of
  mutual obligation — with *mita*, the colonial forced-labor
  draft, standing as the refused opposite already built into the
  lexicon. The register work here starts from vocabulary the
  Andes has maintained for centuries.
- **Institutions exist at every level**: Peru's intercultural
  bilingual education system and official orthographic norms,
  the Cusco academy, university Quechua programs across three
  countries, and Ecuador's unified Kichwa standard — this is a
  marginalized language, not an unresourced one, and the
  partnership question is which door, not whether one exists.
- **Honesty item**: machine translation for Quechua now exists
  in the major systems — and changes nothing about this model;
  register is craft, and authority rests with speakers.

## Who to contact

The variety question (Southern Quechua vs Kichwa vs Bolivian
norms) is settled by who engages — the note below welcomes
redirection, as the Cree track's does.

- **University Quechua programs** — UNSAAC (Cusco) and the
  Andean universities with language departments; PUCP's Quechua
  programs (Lima).
- **Peru's intercultural bilingual education (EIB) apparatus** —
  the Ministry of Education directorate that maintains official
  orthographies and school materials.
- **The Academia Mayor de la Lengua Quechua** (Cusco).
- **Ecuador**: Kichwa education organizations for the unified
  Kichwa standard, if that is the engaging community.
- **Bolivia**: the plurinational language institutes
  (IPELC-affiliated Quechua institute).

>
> I'm writing to ask whether an interface in Quechua — in the

> we are not asking permission to machine-translate the
> language, and we won't do that, whatever tools now exist. The

> whether Quechua speakers — your faculty, your students,

> than one Quechua.

> If this has any place in your work — or if it doesn't, but
> you'd point me somewhere better — I'd be glad for a short
