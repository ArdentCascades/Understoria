# The Taiwan Indigenous languages umbrella — one door for sixteen nations

## What this document is, and deliberately is not

The FirstVoices model (`docs/i18n-partnership-firstvoices.md`),
applied to Taiwan: **sixteen recognized Indigenous (Formosan and
Yami) languages**, national-language status under the Indigenous
Languages Development Act (2017), official romanized
orthographies, certification systems, and a dedicated
**Indigenous Languages Research and Development Foundation**
under the Council of Indigenous Peoples. This is the Austronesian
homeland — the deep ancestry of the family whose Māori and
Hawaiian branches this track has already invited — and the
institutional ground is government-backed in a way nowhere else
on the track is.

Per both track-wide policies: **no Formosan language gets a
scaffold, a published probe, or a candidate-list entry unless
its community chooses it** — official status makes the door
easier to find, not ours to walk through uninvited; each of the
sixteen nations decides for itself.

## What we would offer any community the Council connects us with

The standing model, unchanged: speakers translate, we supply all
tooling, nothing ships before their review; no deadline, any
pace; credit, review authority, unshipping on request theirs;
compensation discussed honestly (or the work living inside the
Foundation's own funded mandate); ownership settled in writing
first. Technically: the official romanizations are Latin-based
and well within the sibling tracks' verified ground, and any
CLDR gaps ride the proven no-ICU pattern.

## Who to contact

- **The Indigenous Languages Research and Development
  Foundation** (財團法人原住民族語言研究發展基金會) — the
  dedicated language body.
- **The Council of Indigenous Peoples** (原住民族委員會) — the
  ministry-level door.
- Through them, as they judge appropriate, the sixteen nations'
  own language promotion organizations.
